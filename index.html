Overview

From asking for help to learning from it

Robots are starting to learn from their own operation, but when an agent should ask a human for help, and what it should change afterward, has no shared formulation or evaluation.

We use physical agentic AI for embodied systems that pursue multi-step goals, monitor their own execution, seek or accept intervention, and turn deployment outcomes into safer behavior. Generalist policies and language planners now give robots such a brain, and robot agents are beginning to improve from teleoperated corrections, fleet deployment, and their own exploration. Some already ask people for help and keep an operator's correction as a rule.

Yet when, whom, and how to ask is left to improvisation; an update may fix one failure while introducing another; and success rate hides human burden, recovery time, and regression. This workshop brings these questions together around one test: does one deployment yield a measurably safer and more capable next one, at what human cost, and without regression?

Key questions

  • ActCan the agent tell that it is failing?
  • AskWhen, whom, and how should it ask for help?
  • LearnWhich part of the agent (plans, verifiers, memory, skills, or policy) should an intervention update?

Outcomes: non-archival papers and demos, a checklist for measuring human burden and regression, and a public roadmap.

Topics

Topics of interest

  1. Help-seekingWhen, whom, and how a robot agent should ask, and how its uncertainty should drive the decision.
  2. Intervention as supervisionTurning human corrections and takeovers into targeted updates for the agent.
  3. Self-monitoringDetecting failure and recognizing what the agent cannot yet do.
  4. Agent memory and skillsWhat an agent stores from deployment: plans, programs, skills, and rules.
  5. Validating updatesChecking that a fix does not introduce regression elsewhere.
  6. Burden-centered evaluationMeasuring human time, recovery, and regression, not only success rate.
  7. Human factorsHow people experience being asked, and how they intervene.

Call for Papers

Submit papers and demos

We invite work on an agent's help-seeking, intervention-driven updates, or their evaluation. Focused work on one of these is welcome.

Research papers4–6 pages

Full research contributions.

Short reports2–4 pages

Position papers, negative results, and deployment reports.

Demos2-page abstract + video

Live or video-backed demos; simulation is welcome.

  • Non-archival: there are no proceedings.
  • Double-blind review on OpenReview, with at least two reviews per submission.
  • Accepted papers are presented as posters; selected papers give spotlight talks.
  • Awards: Best Paper, Best Student Paper, and Best Demo.
  • Submission portal: OpenReview (link will be posted after acceptance).
